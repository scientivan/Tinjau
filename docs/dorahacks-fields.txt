=== Project Description ===

Over 19,000 AI agents are registered on ERC-8004 on Ethereum mainnet, other
agents pay them, and nothing a contract can check says which ones deserve trust.
Read raw, it misleads: of the last 600 reviews, 346 of 367 rated agents had one
reviewer, one wallet wrote 225 of them, and over 60 days clone-scale owners made
83% of 14,771 new registrations.

Tinjau is a verified background check before money moves: a bureau in
Creditcoin's sense, it records and does not judge. Its Creditcoin contract,
GroundedFacts, admits data only through Attestcoin proofs of Ethereum
transactions: which reviews exist, how long each reviewer was active before
reviewing, whether review indices have holes, whether a reviewer owns agents, how
many agents share an owner or minting transaction, and how many attestors were
registered when each fact was admitted. There is no score: consumers pass
thresholds, get admitted evidence plus known gaps, and anyone can replay every
proof from chain data.

AgentHireEscrow prices a hire from those facts and refuses records with holes.
CoverageBounty pays for evidence that changes a decision, in either direction.
GroundedScout picks targets, proves evidence both ways, claims bounties and
hires. Anyone may submit proofs and nobody can invent one: the precompile decides
what is true, a dishonest scout can only withhold, and the registry's own counter
exposes that as a gap. A Gemini-based reader checks payment claims in review
documents against the prover (one agent: 6 claimed, 0 found).

Live on CC3 testnet: 33 Ethereum mainnet transactions admitted, back to 2022 and
still growing; two agents hired at 1%; one gated; a bounty claimed by the scout;
the same proof verified on CC3 mainnet.

Limits: Ethereum mainnet only (Sepolia excluded by design); admitted evidence
with known gaps, not a complete history; the attestor count means registered at
admission, not proof signers.


=== USC Integration Summary ===

Every fact in Tinjau enters through USC. GroundedFacts has no admin, no setter
and no trusted relayer: the only way to change its state is `record`, and every
proof in that call goes through the BlockProver precompile first.

Precompiles used, inside the contract:

- BlockProver 0x...0FD2, `verify`: called on each proof before a single byte of
  it is read. A failed proof reverts with ProofRejected(i).
- BlockProver 0x...0FD2, `calculateTxIndex`: keys each admitted transaction by
  (chainKey, height, txIndex), so duplicates are skipped rather than
  double-counted and several scouts can race safely. It also orders ownership
  transfers exactly by (height, txIndex, logIndex), so the newest proven transfer
  wins whatever order the proofs arrive in.
- AttestorStash 0x...0FD4, `getAttestorsCount`: read on every admitted proof.
  facts().minAttestors reports the lowest attestor count registered for the
  source chain at the moment any of the agent's facts was admitted, and the
  escrow can refuse facts admitted under a thin set (ThinQuorum). Live: 4
  attestors for Ethereum mainnet, 100 CTC bond each.
- ChainInfo 0x...0FD3, `get_latest_attestation_height_and_hash`: attestedTip()
  exposes how far Creditcoin has attested. With facts().coveredThrough a hirer
  can refuse stale facts (Stale).

Decoding and binding. The official EvmV1Decoder from @gluwa/usc-contracts 0.2.0
decodes the proven bytes into sender, receipt status and logs. Inclusion is not
success: logs of a reverted source transaction are never used, though the
sender's activity still counts because they paid gas. A log is used only when its
emitter equals the official ERC-8004 registry configured for that chainKey, so a
look-alike NewFeedback from any other contract is ignored.

Why the product dies without USC. Every fact is derived from Ethereum history. An
EVM contract cannot read its own chain's history, and a contract on another chain
cannot read Ethereum at all. Without the proof, `record` would have to trust its
caller, and Tinjau would become one more signed off-chain aggregator, which is
exactly what ERC-8004 leaves consumers to trust today. With USC, a Creditcoin
contract checks the history itself and can lock money on the result.

Measured gas on `verify`, which tracks continuity roots rather than age: 62,292
at 7 roots, 127,746 at 122 roots, 506,986 at 786 roots for the oldest admitted
transaction (2 March 2022), 631,434 at 984 roots. Roughly 55k plus 580 per root.
Total per admitted proof including decoding and storage is 0.3 to 0.6M gas.

Mainnet readiness, checked read-only: the proof for agent 22771's registration,
fetched from the CC3 mainnet proof builder, returns verify = true from 0x0FD2 on
CC3 mainnet at the same 127,746 gas. Same contract code, same proofs; only the
chainKey in the constructor changes.

Reproducible by anyone. recomputeFromChain() reads every TxAdmitted event, finds
each source transaction on Ethereum, refetches its proof from the Attestcoin
prover and replays it through an off-chain copy of the contract logic. On 13 Sep
2026 it replayed all 33 admitted transactions and produced facts identical to
facts(). The MCP tool tinjau_verify exposes the same check to any agent.

What this does not claim. USC moves trust from RPCs and indexers to Creditcoin's
bonded attestors; it does not remove trust. Only Ethereum mainnet is admitted.
minAttestors is the registered count at admission, not a proof-specific signer
count; coveredThrough is the newest proven relevant height, not a completeness
certificate. Facts are admitted evidence with known gaps, never a complete
history.
