## The problem

Over 19,000 AI agents are registered on ERC-8004 on Ethereum mainnet. Other agents hire and pay them, and nothing a contract can check says which ones deserve the money.

Read raw, the registry misleads. Of the last 600 reviews, **346 of 367** rated agents had exactly one reviewer, and a single wallet wrote **225** reviews across 195 agents. Over 60 days, owners holding ten or more agents made **83%** of 14,771 new registrations. The standard's answer is to aggregate off-chain and trust the aggregator.

## What Tinjau is

A verified background check for an AI agent before money moves. A credit bureau in Creditcoin's original sense: it records, it does not lend and it does not judge.

`GroundedFacts` on Creditcoin admits data **only** through Attestcoin proofs of Ethereum transactions. It stores facts, never opinions: which reviews exist, how long a reviewer was active before reviewing, whether review indices have holes, whether a reviewer owns agents, how many agents share an owner, registrant, URI or minting transaction, and how many attestors were registered when each fact was admitted.

There is no score. A consumer passes its own thresholds and gets **admitted evidence plus known gaps** (never a complete history), and anyone can replay every proof from chain data alone.

## The pieces

- **GroundedFacts**: the bureau. No admin, no upgrade path, no weights.
- **AgentHireEscrow**: prices a hire from those facts and refuses a record with holes. The premium is the agent's cost of credit and goes to its owner.
- **CoverageBounty**: pays for evidence that changes a decision, in either direction. Evidence against an agent pays exactly as much as evidence for it.
- **GroundedScout**: an autonomous agent that picks targets, proves evidence both ways, claims bounties and hires. Anyone may run one.
- **MCP server**: `tinjau_facts`, `tinjau_quote`, `tinjau_verify`, so another agent can ask the bureau before it hires.

Anyone may submit proofs and nobody can invent one. The BlockProver precompile decides what is true, so a dishonest scout can only **withhold** evidence, and the registry's own review counter exposes that as a gap.

## Live on Creditcoin CC3 testnet

- At least **33 Ethereum mainnet transactions** admitted, reaching back to March 2022, and still growing.
- Agent 22771: three reviewers, each active between 97 days and 4 years before their first review, no gaps and no look-alikes. Premium **1%**, hired.
- Agent 50283: one reviewer who owns agents, a missing review index. **Held**. No setting opens it, because a hole is a gate and not a price.
- A bounty funded, then claimed by the scout with proofs that moved the decision.
- The same proof verified on CC3 **mainnet**: `verify = true`, 127,746 gas.
- A Gemini-based reader checked payment claims inside review documents against the prover: on one agent, **6 claimed, 0 found on the chain they name**. That is a report, never a fact on chain.

49 contract tests. Every number recomputable off-chain from the same proofs.

## Limits, stated up front

- Ethereum mainnet only. Sepolia is excluded by design; Base, where most ERC-8004 activity lives, is out of reach today.
- Admitted evidence with known gaps, not a complete history.
- The attestor count means attestors registered at admission, not the signers behind a particular proof.
- Attestcoin moves trust to Creditcoin's bonded attestors; it does not remove it.
- Evidence Exchange: paying only for a decision change and source-order conflict resolution are live. Batch claim, adjudication receipt and same-transaction re-pricing are not built.
- No consumer contract on Creditcoin yet; the escrow is the worked example.
