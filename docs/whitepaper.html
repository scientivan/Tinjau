Tinjau

Verified background checks for AI agents: facts about ERC-8004 agents and their reviewers, proven from Ethereum into Creditcoin through the Attestcoin Protocol.

Whitepaper v1.0 · 14 September 2026 · Dien Muhammad Scientivan Kurniapramono · BUIDL CTC 2026 Fall · tinjau.xyz · github.com/scientivan/Tinjau

Abstract

Over 19,000 AI agents are registered on ERC-8004 on Ethereum mainnet and already hire and pay each other. Nothing a contract can check says which of them deserve the money. Read raw, the registry misleads: most rated agents carry a single reviewer, one wallet wrote hundreds of reviews, and most recent registrations come from owners operating at clone scale. The standard's own answer is to aggregate off-chain and trust the aggregator.

Tinjau is a bureau in Creditcoin's original sense. It records and it does not judge. Its Creditcoin contract GroundedFacts admits data only through Attestcoin proofs of Ethereum transactions, verified by the BlockProver precompile inside the transaction that writes them. It stores facts, never opinions, and it emits no score. A consumer passes its own thresholds and receives admitted evidence together with the gaps that are known to exist, and anyone can replay every proof from chain data alone and arrive at the same numbers.

2. Why the existing answer is not enough

ERC-8004 states that aggregation will happen off-chain. In practice that means an indexer reads the registry, computes a reputation, and signs it. A contract that wants to act on that number must trust the indexer, its RPC provider, and its uptime. The trust simply moves, and it moves to a party with no bond and no penalty for being wrong.

The obstacle is structural rather than one of effort. An EVM contract cannot read its own chain's history, and a contract on a different chain cannot read Ethereum at all. Any on-chain consumer is therefore forced back onto a signed assertion from somebody.

3. Design principles

  1. Facts, not scores. The contract computes no weight and issues no verdict. Value comes from refusing to judge, because the party carrying the risk is the one entitled to set the bar.
  2. Only proofs enter. Every state change passes through verify on the BlockProver precompile. There is no admin, no setter, no trusted relayer and no upgrade path.
  3. Omission must never pay. A reviewer counts as grounded only when every one of their review indices is proven, so withholding evidence can never improve an agent's standing.
  4. Logs only from the official registries. An event is read only when its emitter matches the ERC-8004 registry configured for that source chain.
  5. No language model in the fact path. A model reads claims and produces reports; it never writes a fact.
  6. Everything recomputable. Any number the contract reports can be reproduced off-chain from the same proofs.

4. How a fact enters

A submitter, called a scout, finds an Ethereum transaction that carries evidence, fetches an Attestcoin proof for it, and calls record. Inside that call, and before a single byte of the payload is read, the contract does the following.

record(Proof[]) for each proof: BlockProver 0x..0FD2 verify(chainKey, height, encodedTx, merkleProof, continuityProof) rejected -> revert ProofRejected(i) BlockProver 0x..0FD2 calculateTxIndex(merkleProof) key = (chainKey, height, txIndex) duplicate -> skip, never revert EvmV1Decoder decode sender, receipt status, logs status == 0 -> logs discarded, sender activity still counts emitter != registry -> log ignored AttestorStash 0x..0FD4 getAttestorsCount(chainKey) stored alongside the fact ChainInfo 0x..0FD3 attested tip, exposed as attestedTip(chainKey)

Keying by (chainKey, height, txIndex) lets several independent scouts race without double counting, and it orders ownership transfers exactly by height, transaction index and log index, so the newest proven transfer wins regardless of the order in which proofs arrive.

Why this is load bearing. Remove Attestcoin and record would have to trust its caller. Tinjau would become one more signed off-chain aggregator, which is the thing it exists against. With it, a Creditcoin contract checks Ethereum history itself and can lock money on the result.

Two field names are routinely misread, so we state them plainly. minAttestors is the network context at admission, not the set of signers behind a particular proof. coveredThrough is the newest proven fact, not a certificate that every block up to the tip was examined.

6. Turning facts into a price

AgentHireEscrow is an example consumer, not the product. It reads facts() under the hirer's own thresholds and computes a premium, which is the agent's cost of credit and is paid to the agent's owner.

coverage = min(10000, breadthGrounded * 10000 / k) cloneFactor = c * 10000 / (c + cloneDensityLB) risk = 10000 - coverage * cloneFactor / 10000 premium = baseBps + (maxBps - baseBps) * risk / 10000

The premium is computed, never configured. A live example: agent 50724 carries a risk of 7,223 basis points, which yields a premium of 1,472 basis points, or 14.72%, sitting between the 1% floor and the 20% ceiling.

A record with holes is not priced at all. If gapCount exceeds zero the escrow reverts with Gated. Missing evidence is a gate, never a price, because otherwise an agent could buy its way past its own silence. The escrow can also refuse a truncated answer, facts admitted under a thin attestor set (ThinQuorum), and facts whose newest proven height is too far behind the attested tip (Stale).

7. Paying for evidence, in either direction

CoverageBounty lets anyone put money on a question. fund snapshots a four-part decision vector under the funder's thresholds: are there enough verified reviewers, are there no holes, are look-alikes under the limit, and is there any negative review. proveAndClaim admits new proofs and pays the full bounty only if one of those four predicates changed within that call.

Evidence that damages an agent pays exactly as much as evidence that helps it. Proof that changes nothing pays nothing and costs only gas. This is what makes the bureau self-filling without a subsidy: the people who care about an answer fund the evidence that settles it.

8. The scout

GroundedScout is an autonomous agent that decides what is worth proving. It logs four decisions on every cycle.

  • R1 targeting. Open bounties first, then agents that were requested, then the most reviewed agents of the last seven days.
  • R2 evidence both ways. Complete records of senior reviewers, and against the agent: negative reviews, revocations, reviewers who own agents, clone siblings, and the highest review index.
  • R3 timing and cost. Skip what is already admitted; prove only when a bounty covers the gas; batch four proofs per transaction.
  • R4 acting. Hire through the escrow when the facts clear its own thresholds, otherwise fund a bounty.

Anyone may run one. The scout has no privilege: it carries proofs and nothing else. A dishonest scout cannot insert a review that never happened, because the precompile rejects anything not included in an attested block and logs count only from the official registries. All it can do is withhold, and the registry's own per-pair index counter exposes that as gapCount. Contributions change completeness; they never change correctness.

9. Verifiability

recomputeFromChain() reads every TxAdmitted event from the bureau, locates each source transaction on Ethereum by block and index, fetches its proof again from the Attestcoin prover, and replays it through an off-chain copy of the contract logic. On 13 September 2026 it replayed all 33 admitted transactions and produced facts identical to facts() for agents 22771, 21548, 50283 and 50286. The MCP tool tinjau_verify exposes the same check to any agent that can speak the Model Context Protocol.

10. Reading claims, outside the fact path

Review documents assert payments in marketplace-specific formats that no schema covers. A Gemini model reads them and proposes (network, txHash) pairs, falling to the next model in a ladder when a quota runs out. Deterministic checks then decide: the hash must appear in the document, the chain must be readable by Attestcoin, and the prover together with the precompile must confirm inclusion. On agent 50283 the reader found six claimed payment transactions and none of them exists on the chain it names.

That output is a report. It is never written on chain, and no price depends on it.

12. Trust assumptions

  • Attestcoin moves trust away from RPC providers and indexers and onto Creditcoin's bonded attestor set. It does not remove trust. On mainnet the minimum bond is currently zero, and a consumer that cares can refuse facts admitted under a thin set.
  • The precompile is trusted to verify inclusion correctly. Tinjau does not re-implement it.
  • The registry addresses are constructor arguments with no admin, so they are fixed at deployment and visible to anyone reading the contract.
  • An agent's own registration document is the owner's unverified claim. The interface labels it as such and never mixes it with proven facts.

13. Limits, stated up front

  • Ethereum mainnet only. Sepolia is excluded by design, because a registry entry there costs nothing to mint and admitting it would let anyone fabricate a record for free. Base, where most ERC-8004 activity now lives, is not readable by Attestcoin today.
  • Admitted evidence with known gaps, never a complete history. Holes below the highest proven index are visible; a newer review that nobody has proven is not.
  • Clone density is a lower bound. Aged wallets can be bought, and honest multi-agent operators look like clone farms. The contract reports the count and draws no conclusion.
  • No independent consumer contract on Creditcoin yet. The escrow is a worked example of one.
  • Evidence Exchange is partly live. The decision-change payout and source-order conflict resolution work today. Batch claim, an adjudication receipt recording the decision before and after with the predicate that flipped, and hire re-pricing inside the same transaction are not built.

14. What comes next

  1. Finish Evidence Exchange: batch claim, the adjudication receipt, and same-transaction re-pricing.
  2. Consumer contracts beyond our own escrow. Any marketplace, lender or agent can already read facts() through IAgentFacts.
  3. Additional source chains as Attestcoin reaches them. The registry table is a constructor argument, so no rewrite is required.
  4. Distribution through the Model Context Protocol, so an agent consults the bureau before it hires rather than after it has paid.

Who pays. The hirer, as a premium on the job, and the premium goes to the agent's owner. That is deliberate: it makes a clean, fully proven record an asset its owner has a reason to protect.
